A calm, cozy town you can pick up in a minute.

This is the visual reference for making new art, cards and screens for Tile RPG. Everything on this page is rendered with the game's own stylesheets, sprites and card data, so it matches the game exactly. Flip between Dark and Light at the top: every asset must work in both.

Jump to: Colour for hex codes, Cards for the template and the full catalogue of file names, Assets for sizes and delivery.

Principles

The game is a quiet, soft, rounded world. These rules explain most design decisions.

Calm over loud

Soft sea-glass teal, warm sand and muted gold. Nothing flashes or screams. Effects are slow and small. Danger is a dusty red, never neon.

Rounded everything

Pills for buttons and chips, 12 to 22 px corners on panels and cards, a 28 px sheet. No hard corners and no thin sharp strokes.

Mobile first

Designed on a 400 x 860 phone. Tap targets are at least 44 px. Art is seen small: a card icon is 26 to 42 px on screen, so silhouettes beat detail.

Two themes, one art set

Dark is "Slate glass", light is "Sand and sea glass". Art sits on coloured card gradients and both backgrounds, so avoid pure white or pure black fringes.

Tokens, not hex

In code every colour is a CSS variable. New UI never hard-codes a colour; art is the one place to bring your own palette, guided by the swatches below.

Friendly and legible

Plain words, big numbers, one clear action per screen. Serif display type for titles, round sans for everything else.

Icons

The game currently uses three icon systems. New art can replace any of them slot by slot.

1. Emoji (cards, tabs, keywords, UI)

Every card, tab, keyword, weather and button icon is an emoji today. Where a card has art, the picture replaces the emoji at the same size (1em). Keyword icons come from the engine and are shown live:

2. Town sprites (SVG symbols in assets/sprites.js)

24 hand-drawn SVG symbols. To restyle, keep each id and viewBox. Props, trees and ground pieces are 64 x 64; buildings are 128 x 128 (the bakery is 192 x 128). The tile on the map is 48 px, so these are drawn at 0.75x.

3. App icon

Currently a placeholder (a card emoji on a green square) at assets/icon-180.png, icon-192.png, icon-512.png and a 32 px favicon. Replace these four files with the final logo; nothing else changes.

Icon rules for new art

The game adds a faint drop shadow to card art and to static UI icons (dock tabs, list and panel icons, keyword icons), sized with the icon and tuned per theme. Don't bake a shadow into icon art.

Do

  • Bold, simple silhouettes with soft rounded edges
  • 2 or 3 tones plus a highlight; gentle gradients are fine
  • Keep the subject in the central 80% of the canvas
  • Check at 32 px and on both themes

Don't

  • Thin outlines, tiny details or text inside icons
  • Pure white or pure black edges that vanish on one theme
  • Neon or high-saturation colours
  • A baked-in card frame, border or rarity colour

Cards

A card is a rarity-coloured face the game draws for you, with your picture in the middle. You supply only the picture (and, for new cards, the name and stats). This section explains every part: the numbers, the rarities, the four families, the types, the keywords, how to compose the art, a checker to test your own file, and the complete catalogue with file names.

Anatomy and sizes

1Cost: a round badge hanging off the top-left corner (22 px, --ink fill, --stone number). Cards cost 0 to 5.
2Art: your picture, centred, drawn at 1em inside the icon area. This is the only part you make.
3Name: one line, white, centred, cut off with an ellipsis if it is too long.
4Keyword icons: up to three small emoji (see Keywords). Spells show a "Spell" tag instead.
5Stats: power (sword) left, health (heart) right, bold white with a soft shadow.
The art canvas

512 x 512 px, PNG, transparent background, no frame. The teal square is the safe area (410 x 410): keep the subject inside it. The dotted circle is the zone for round subjects; the pink line is a ground line for standing creatures. Download: template.svg (import into Figma, hide the GUIDES group before export).

Rarities

Rarity sets the face colour, the glow, and how special the card feels. It is not a different canvas: the art area, size and rules are the same, but the amount of care and richness should rise with rarity. Examples below are real cards.

Families

Every creature belongs to one of four families (spells and a few neutral creatures belong to none). A family is tied to a district, gives deck bonuses at 6 or 8 cards, and shapes which keywords it favours. Colour and motif notes are suggestions for art direction: the game does not tint cards by family, so the cue has to come from your drawing.

Types and variants

Keywords

A keyword is a rule shown as one small emoji on the card. It is a good source of visual ideas: art that hints at the ability helps players read a card at a glance. Counts and examples are live.

Composition templates

Six ways to place a subject on the canvas. Each is shown with the template guides (teal = safe area, dotted = circle zone, pink = ground line) and at 32 px. Draw your own versions of these shapes; they are examples of placement, not a style to copy.

The same creature drawn well and then with six typical mistakes. The small chips show each at 32 px on the common and mythic faces, the way a player sees it.

    Names, stats and writing

    Names
    LengthAim for 12 characters or fewer. The longest in the game is ; names over about 12 characters are cut off with an ellipsis on the battle card and wrap to two lines on tiles.
    StylePlain, concrete nouns people can picture: Sprout, Pebble, Deep Current, Aurora Stag. One to three words, no numbers or punctuation.
    CaseTitle Case, no trailing full stop.
    Id (file name)Lowercase, hyphens, no spaces: aurora-stag. The id never changes once a card exists, even if the name does.
    Longest names today (check these still fit)
    Stats ranges that exist today

    Cost curve: commons are 1 to 2 energy, mythics 4 to 5. Stats rise with cost. A card's numbers are balanced by simulation, so when adding cards, copy the numbers of an existing card of the same role and rarity rather than inventing new ones.

    Ability text

    Creatures have no written ability: their rules are their keywords (see above). Spells have one sentence of effect text in plain words, starting with a verb: "Deal 2 damage to an enemy card." "Draw 2 cards." The longest today is characters.

    Card backs and sleeves

    The face-down side of every card (deck piles and the opponent's hand) is drawn by the game. Players can buy cosmetic sleeves that re-skin the back of their own cards. These are CSS today; if you want to design new sleeves, tell me the pattern and colours.

    Catalogue and file names

    Every card in the game with the file name its art must have. Click a card for details, or click the file name to copy it. The same list with tick boxes is ART-LIST.md; after the story retheme it will be regenerated from the new names.

    Delivery checklist

    World and scenes

    Four districts, each with a biome palette, music chord set and boss. Interiors have a stage, a sign, a speech bubble and an action sheet.

    DistrictBiomeFamilyBossMood

    Town time and weather

    The town re-tints through the day (dawn, midday, dusk, night) and by season (foliage colours) and weather (clear, cloudy, rain, storm, snow, with particle overlays). Art for the map should be drawn in the neutral "midday" look; the game tints it. Building interiors use four themes: warm (wood), cool (blue), dark (cellar) and the cellar's own torch-lit stage, each with wall, floor, rug and counter colours that lighten in the light theme.

    Reference screens

    Real screenshots of the current game, dark on the left and light on the right of each pair, for context when designing new assets. (Captured at 400 x 860.)

    Motion and sound

    Motion

    Dialog pop-in.4 s, cubic-bezier(.34, 1.56, .64, 1) (a gentle overshoot)
    Tab change.3 s, cubic-bezier(.22, .8, .3, 1), slides 16 px
    Pressscale to .94 to .96
    Sheetsrise .48 s; leave .24 s
    RuleOnly opacity and transform move. Everything is switched off by Calm mode and by the device's reduced-motion setting

    Sound

    All sounds are generated live from sine waves, never recorded: soft wooden taps, glass bells, water drops and gentle noise. Volumes are low and ring-outs short; the everyday chime is one quiet note. The music is a slow evolving pad in a pentatonic-friendly key, with a different chord family per season and a different voicing per district. If you commission audio, match this: warm, round, unobtrusive, no sharp attacks.

    Figma checklist

    1. One 512 x 512 frame per card, named with the card id from the catalogue above.
    2. Draw inside the safe area; keep a transparent background.
    3. Preview on the five rarity gradients and on dark and light backgrounds; check 32 px.
    4. Export PNG at 1x, optimise if over 60 KB.
    5. Send or commit the files; the id is added to the art list, the checklist updates, and it is published.
